# Product

## Signals

The question is not how to build something but whether, for whom, or when: scope, priority, a default that shapes what users do, a limit or price, which users get a feature, what happens to existing data or customers when behaviour changes. Engineering evidence alone cannot settle it; it rests on what the product is for.

## Research

- Read what the project already says about its goals: specs, tickets, `CLAUDE.md`, product docs, earlier decisions in the conversation. A stated goal often settles what looked open.
- Research domain practice and user expectation on the web: how established products in this domain handle it, and any standard or regulation that applies. Scale it to the stakes: one or two sources for a reversible default, more for something customers will rely on. Keep each link for `recommendation.sources`.

## Skill to consult

None by default. When the installed skill list has one whose description fits the domain in question, consult it with the Skill tool, passing the question and what you found.

## Attributes and options

Every option carries these attributes:

- **Who is affected**: which users, customers or teams, and how.
- **Cost of reversal**: what undoing it later would take, for example a migration, a message to customers, a broken habit.
- **Not decided by this**: what stays open after choosing it, so the owner does not read more into the choice than it holds.

Recommend even when the owner must decide: the reason says which goal the pick serves and what evidence supports it.
